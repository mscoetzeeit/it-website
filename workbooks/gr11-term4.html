LEARN IT

From web pages to the Internet of Things

Web 1.0 (1990s) pages were mostly read-only. Web 2.0 is the read-write web of blogs, wikis and social media, where users create the content. Web 3.0 is the emerging “intelligent” web that tries to understand the meaning of data. The internet has also grown from connecting people to connecting things.

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical objects that have sensors, a small processor and an internet connection. Its characteristics: each device has its own network address; it senses something in the real world; it sends and receives data automatically, without a person typing; and it can often be monitored or controlled from far away. Uses: smart homes, wearables, remote health monitoring, soil sensors on farms, connected cars and smart cities. Risks: privacy, hacking of unprotected devices, and dependence on electricity and connectivity (load shedding switches many of them off).
LEARN IT

Big Data and the Fourth Industrial Revolution

Big Data is data that is too large, too fast or too mixed for an ordinary spreadsheet or single database to handle. Think of Mama Dlamini's exercise book against a supermarket chain that records every barcode scan, card payment, truck position and customer post. The three Vs describe the difference.
VMeaningExample
VolumeThe amount of data, from terabytes upwardA mobile network stores billions of call records
VelocityThe speed at which data arrives and must be processedA bank checks each card swipe for fraud in a fraction of a second
VarietyMany forms: tables, text, images, video, GPS and sensor dataA hospital keeps X-rays, doctors' notes and heart-rate readings
Industrial revolutions: 1st steam and water power; 2nd electricity and assembly lines; 3rd electronics, computers and automation; 4th (4IR) merges the physical, digital and biological worlds through AI, robotics, IoT, Big Data and 3D printing. Employment: routine jobs (till operators, assembly work) can be automated and disappear, while new jobs such as data analyst and robot technician appear, so workers must retrain. Society: life becomes more convenient and healthcare and education reach remote places, but people without devices or data are left behind (the digital divide) and more data about everyone is collected, which threatens privacy.

Download, stream, broadcast, VOD and IPTV

Sipho downloads a match overnight; Lerato streams it, and load shedding switches her router off halfway. Only Sipho can keep watching. When you download, the whole file is copied to your device, so you keep it and can use it offline. When you stream, data is played as it arrives and no copy is kept; a few seconds are held in a buffer so playback does not stutter, and it needs a steady connection and a lot of bandwidth. A live broadcast is streamed as it happens (a match, a news bulletin) so you cannot skip ahead. Video on demand (VOD) lets you choose what to watch and when from a library on servers (Netflix, Showmax). IPTV delivers TV channels over an internet connection instead of an aerial or dish, with pause, rewind and catch-up.
LEARN IT

Compression

Compression makes a file smaller so it uses less space and travels faster. Lossless compression keeps every bit, so the original can be rebuilt exactly (ZIP, PNG). Lossy compression permanently throws away detail people are unlikely to notice, so the file is much smaller but the original cannot be rebuilt (MP3 audio, JPEG photos, MPEG-4 video). More compression means a smaller file but lower quality.
Picture size = width × height (pixels) × 3 bytes per pixel (true colour). Saving (%) = (original − compressed) ÷ original × 100. A ratio of 15:1 means the compressed file is one fifteenth of the original.
LEARN IT

Websites and secure logins

A static website shows the same fixed pages to everyone and changes only when the developer edits the files; it is quick and cheap. A dynamic website builds each page when asked, usually from a database, so it can show your name, cart or today's prices. HTTP sends pages as plain text that anyone intercepting them can read; HTTPS encrypts the data (SSL/TLS) and shows a padlock, so any page that takes a password or card number must use it.
Multi-factor authentication (MFA) asks for proofs from two different groups: something you know (password), have (phone, token) or are (fingerprint). A one-time PIN (OTP) works once and only for a few minutes. A security token (a device or app) shows a new code every 30 to 60 seconds. A stolen password alone is then useless, although SMS codes can be intercepted and a lost phone is a problem.

LEARN IT

Online identity, footprint and location

Your online identity is the picture of you that others build from your profiles, posts and comments. Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave. An active footprint is data you share on purpose (posts, forms); a passive footprint is collected without you doing anything (cookies, your IP address, location logs). Content can be copied and stored by others, and employers, universities and criminals look at it. Protect yourself: keep profiles private; think before you post; never share your ID number, address or holiday dates; use strong, different passwords and MFA; check app permissions; log out on shared computers. POPIA gives you rights over your personal information.

Location-based services (LBS) use the position of your device, found with GPS satellites, cell towers or Wi-Fi: navigation, ride-hailing, food delivery, local weather, “find my phone”. Benefits: convenience, safety and recovery of lost phones. Risks: your movements can be tracked, sold to advertisers or used for stalking, and criminals can learn when a house is empty. Allow location only while an app is in use.
LEARN IT

Careers, globalisation and Big Data

Web designer: plans the look and feel. Web developer: writes the HTML, CSS and code. Multimedia designer: creates graphics, animation, audio and video. Database administrator: secures, backs up and tunes databases. SEO specialist: helps a site rank higher in search results. Cybersecurity analyst: watches for attacks and protects systems.

Globalisation means people and countries are more closely linked by trade, travel and the internet. A crafts-person in Khayelitsha can sell overseas and families stay in touch across continents, but local shops can lose customers to giant online stores, some jobs move to cheaper countries and local cultures can be pushed aside. Big Data and IoT bring better healthcare and smarter cities, but also constant surveillance, profiling and data breaches, which is why laws such as POPIA matter.

TopicWhat to remember
Hardware and OSCPU runs instructions; cache is tiny, very fast memory beside it. RAM is volatile working memory; ROM permanently holds the BIOS, which runs the POST at start-up. An SSD (flash, no moving parts) is faster than a HDD but costs more per GB. The operating system manages memory, files, devices (via drivers), users and security, and provides the interface.
NetworksIn a star network one cable fault affects one PC, but a switch fault stops everyone. SMTP sends e-mail, POP3 downloads it, IMAP keeps it on the server; FTP moves large files; HTTP/HTTPS carry web pages; VoIP carries calls.
SecurityMalware: virus (needs a file), worm (spreads alone), trojan (disguised), rootkit (hidden), ransomware, spyware, adware. Attacks: phishing, pharming, spoofing, SQL injection. Remedies: antivirus, firewall, strong passwords, access rights, encryption, updates, MFA, UPS and the 3-2-1 backup rule.
Databases and data qualityA primary key is unique; a foreign key holds another table's primary key. Relationships: 1:1, 1:M, and M:M (solved with a link table). Referential integrity: a foreign key must match an existing primary key. GIGO: wrong input gives wrong output. Validation (by the program) checks that data is reasonable; verification checks that it matches its source.
