Internet & E-Communication (T3) Grade 12

Grade 12 extends internet knowledge: video conferencing, advanced browser features, blogs/vlogs/podcasts, GPS and geo-tagging, social networks, mobile devices as communication tools.

Term 3

Video Conferencing

Video conferencing allows multiple people to see and hear each other simultaneously using Internet connections (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet).

AdvantagesDisadvantages
No travel required — saves time and moneyRequires reliable internet; poor connection = poor quality
Connect globally in real timeLack of physical presence — harder to read body language
Record meetings for later reviewTechnical problems (audio/video failures)
Share screens and collaborate on documentsSecurity risks — "Zoom bombing" (uninvited guests)

Good Practices for Video Conferencing

  • Test audio and video before the meeting starts
  • Mute when not speaking to reduce background noise
  • Use a professional background or virtual background
  • Look into the camera, not at your own image
  • Use waiting rooms and passwords to prevent uninvited guests
  • Use the hand-raise feature rather than interrupting
Term 3

Web Browser Features — Advanced

Bookmarks / Favourites
Save frequently visited URLs for quick access; organised in folders
History
List of recently visited websites; useful for finding a page you forgot to bookmark; can be cleared for privacy
Home page settings
Set a specific page (or blank page) to open when the browser starts or when you click the home button
Blocking websites
Parents/schools can block specific URLs or categories of sites (parental controls, content filtering)
Caching
Browser saves copies of web pages/images locally so they load faster on repeat visits; can cause issues with outdated content (Ctrl+Shift+Delete to clear)
Browser extensions
Add-ons that extend browser functionality; installed from the browser's extension store
Private browsing
Incognito (Chrome) / InPrivate (Edge) — browsing history, cookies and form data NOT saved on the device; still visible to ISP and websites

Extension typePurposeExample

URL SHORTENER

A tool or service, such as TinyURL.com, which converts a long URL to a shorter version. This shorter version of the URL will take a user to the same web address, but is easier to remember and/or type out.

Biometrics

Biometrics uses a person's unique physical or behavioural characteristics to verify their identity. It is commonly used to unlock smartphones, log into banking apps, and control access to secure buildings.

Fingerprint
Scans the unique pattern of ridges on a finger; common on smartphones and laptops
Face (facial recognition)
Uses a camera to recognise and match unique facial features
Iris/Retina (eye scanning)
Scans the unique patterns in the iris or retina of the eye; highly accurate
Palm (palm print/vein scanning)
Scans the pattern of a palm or the vein patterns beneath the skin
Voice (voice recognition)
Recognises a person's unique voice patterns and speech characteristics
Term 3

File/Data Synchronisation

When a file is "synced", it is automatically kept identical and up to date across multiple devices or with a cloud service. For example, if you edit a file on your phone, it automatically updates on your laptop too. This is related to, but not the same as, cloud storage — synchronisation is the process that keeps files up to date between devices and the cloud.

Latest version available
The most recent version of a file is always available on every connected device
No manual copying needed
Files update automatically without transferring them yourself (e.g. via USB flash drive)
Automatic backup
A synced copy in the cloud serves as a backup if a device is lost, stolen or damaged
Seamless continuation
You can stop working on one device and continue on another without losing anything
Term 3

Public Wi-Fi — Security

Risks of Public Wi-Fi

  • Data can be intercepted by other users on the same network, since public Wi-Fi is often unencrypted
  • Attackers can set up fake hotspots that look like the real network to steal data
  • Increased risk of malware distribution over unsecured networks

Safeguards

  • Avoid accessing sensitive information (banking, personal accounts) on public Wi-Fi
  • Use a VPN to encrypt your connection
  • Ensure websites use HTTPS before entering information
  • Turn off file sharing while on public networks
  • Forget the network on your device after use

Internet of Things and Emerging Technologies

Internet of Things (IoT)
This refers to the trend whereby all sorts of objects and devices are increasingly being connected to one another via the internet. This can range from surveillance systems to geysers, washing machines, 'smart' vehicles and traffic lights. Various sensors in the devices can produce data for all sorts of purposes, including diagnostics and running systems more efficiently.
Autonomous vehicles
A self-driving car, also known as an autonomous vehicle, driverless car, or robo-car is a vehicle that can sense its environment and move safely with little or no human input.
Drone technology
A drone, is an unmanned aircraft. Essentially, a drone is a flying robot that can be remotely controlled or fly autonomously through software-controlled flight plans in their embedded systems, working in conjunction with on-board sensors and GPS. They are widely used in agriculture, photography, game ranging, parcel deliveries, etc.

Term 3

GPS and Geo-Tagging

GPS (Global Positioning System)
Uses satellites to determine exact location (latitude and longitude) anywhere on Earth. Used in: navigation apps, fleet tracking, fitness trackers, emergency services.
Geo-tagging
Embedding GPS coordinates into a file (photo, social media post, tweet). When you take a photo on a phone with location services on, the exact coordinates are saved in the image metadata.
PRIVACY RISK — Geo-tagging

Sharing a geo-tagged photo online reveals exactly WHERE you were. This can be exploited by stalkers, burglars (knowing you're not home) or others with bad intentions. Always check location settings before sharing photos publicly.

EXAM TIP — Private Browsing

A common misconception: private browsing is NOT fully anonymous. Your ISP, employer, school network administrator and the websites you visit can still see what you're doing. It only prevents your local device from saving history, cookies and form data. Use a VPN for actual privacy from your ISP.

Common Exam Mistakes

Incognito / private browsing makes you anonymous online.
It only stops your device saving history; your ISP and the websites still see you.
Downloading and streaming are the same.
Downloading saves a full copy; streaming plays in real time without saving.
A bookmark and the browser history are the same.
A bookmark is a link you deliberately saved; history is an automatic record of sites you visited.
