Storing Data Online

  • Cookies — small text files stored on the user's device by a website. Remember preferences, login sessions, shopping carts.
  • Online databases — data stored on remote servers. Examples: Gmail messages, your social media posts.
  • Online applications — programs that run over the internet rather than being installed on your local computer; they store data, execute instructions and present output entirely through the browser or app. Examples: Google Docs, Gmail, internet banking apps.

Exam Technique — "Explain the Role of a Scripting Language..."

A very common NSC question style names a specific business or website and asks you to explain the role of a scripting language, such as PHP or JavaScript, on the [Business Name] website. A weak answer just recites the definition of JavaScript or PHP in general. A strong answer roots the explanation in that specific website — say which type of scripting you're describing, what it generally does, and then give a concrete example of it happening on the business in the question.

Worked Example — "...on the BSHS Online Store"

Client-side scripting (e.g. JavaScript) runs in the customer's own browser, so it can react instantly without waiting for the server. On the BSHS Online Store it could:

  • Validate the checkout form before it is submitted — e.g. checking that a cellphone number field isn't empty or that the quantity entered is a valid positive number — so the customer sees the error immediately, without a round trip to the server.
  • Update the shopping cart total the instant a customer changes a quantity, with no need to reload the page.
  • Show or hide extra product information (e.g. a "Read more" description) when clicked.

Server-side scripting (e.g. PHP) runs on the store's web server, so it's the only place that can safely reach the database. On the BSHS Online Store it could:

  • Check the product database to confirm an item is still in stock before allowing checkout.
  • Calculate the order total and generate an order confirmation.
  • Securely process the customer's login and payment details.
  • Insert the new order into the database and update the stock level once payment succeeds.
Adapt this to any scenario

The business name in the question changes every year, but the underlying roles don't: client-side scripting is always about instant, in-browser feedback (validation, updating what's on screen, no server round trip), and server-side scripting is always about the things only the server can do safely (checking/updating a database, authentication, processing payments). Re-read the scenario, pick one or two of its specific features (a cart, a login, a booking form, a search bar), and describe the role in terms of that feature rather than giving a generic definition.
